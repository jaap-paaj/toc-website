Eight out of ten AI pilots end without a follow-up. Not because the technology fails, but because nobody asks the hard questions.

## The real problem with AI is not an AI problem

Organisations invest in pilots, write tidy evaluations and everyone nods. And then, usually, nothing happens. No scaling, no redesigned roles, no sharp decisions. Pilots become zombies: not alive enough to grow, [not dead enough to bury](/en/blog/zombie-pilots).

This week the European Commission launched its AI Continent Action Plan. Billions for compute infrastructure, foundation models, AI gigafactories. Good news. But the plan is about building, not about users. About compute, not about adoption.

What sits beneath almost every stalled pilot is the same thing:

- Nobody has said out loud which roles change if this actually works
- Success is measured in efficiency, not in better work
- Leadership claims the narrative but does not invest in what comes next

The question nobody asks: if this actually works, who loses what and who gains what? Only when you dare to have that conversation do you know whether you have an adoption problem or a trust problem.

## What if efficiency is the problem

A marketing team of 30. AI tool purchased for content creation. Vendor says: 60% faster. Team says: the work has become harder.

Automating the easy work sounds like a good idea. Briefings, first drafts, standard formats. What remains is only the difficult and intensive: strategy, client conversations, complex campaigns. If you are not careful, the new normal is eight hours a day without variation. That looks considerably smarter than it is.

If you only measure what gets faster, you miss what breaks:

- The variation people need to enjoy their work
- The learning moments embedded in simple tasks
- The difference between more efficient work and [better work](/en/blog/meaning-test)

Do not just measure how much time AI saves. Also check whether the work that remains is still worth doing. After all, a team that burns out 40% faster is no use to anyone.

## Frequently asked questions about AI problems

**Why do AI projects fail?**
Most AI projects fail organisationally, not technically. Ownership is missing after the pilot phase, success criteria are vague, and nobody forces a decision. The result: projects that run indefinitely without direction. [Read more about Zombie Pilots](/en/blog/zombie-pilots).

**How do you recognise Shadow AI in your organisation?**
If employees are using AI tools on personal accounts, you have Shadow AI. This is not a compliance problem. It is valuable intelligence about where friction is unbearable. The solution is not to ban it but to professionalise it. [Read more about Shadow AI](/en/blog/shadow-ai).

**What is the Meaning Test?**
The Meaning Test asks a fundamental question: has the work improved, or just gotten faster? If the work that remains after AI is less meaningful, you have optimised the wrong thing. [Read more about the Meaning Test](/en/blog/meaning-test).

**How do you start an AI strategy?**
Not with technology, but with the work. Go and look at where the friction is, where people get stuck, where time is lost on tasks that add no value. Start small, prove the value, and only scale when the evidence is there. [Read more about AI strategy](/en/blog/wat-is-ai-strategie).

**What is AI First Aid?**
A free tool that helps you identify what is going wrong with AI in your organisation. You describe your situation, receive a diagnosis based on common patterns, and get concrete actions to work with. [Try it here](/en/ai-ehbo).
